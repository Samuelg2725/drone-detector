 How detection works

                    

                        
1. Sweeping

                        
Each sensor (HackRF or Pluto) captures 20 MHz at a time and keeps the clean middle 14 MHz, retuning until each band is
                        covered: 868 MHz, 2.4 GHz, 5.165-5.255 GHz and 5.72-5.855 GHz - about once a second. Each slice is ~128 averaged FFTs, so the
                        noise is smooth. Every signal more than 6 dB above the local noise floor is measured: centre, width, how peaked it is (crest
                        factor), how its edges fall away (edge drop), its duty cycle and its airtime.

                        
For the first ~25 s it learns what is normal here (WiFi routers etc.) - keep drones off then.


                        
2. The rules, and the confidence each gives

                        
RuleWhat it looks forConfidence

                        
Frequency-hopping video link (2.4 / 5 GHz)A strong (≥15 dB), wide (≥3 MHz) signal in 4 of the last 5 sweeps, peaking on 3+ frequencies, at least 2 of them normally quiet here. WiFi routers don't jump around; this kind of drone link does. No calibration needed.85%

                        
FHSS control link (868 MHz)Short packets in 5 of the last 10 sweeps, on 6+ channels spread over 2+ MHz (ExpressLRS / Crossfire). Alarms, meters and LoRaWAN use one or a few fixed channels. Not yet tested on a real link.80%

                        
Control + video at onceBoth of the above (or a video match) at the same sensor - the strongest sign of an FPV drone.92%

                        
Analog FPV video (shape)≥5 MHz wide, peaked in the middle (crest ≥7 dB), tapering edges (edge drop ≥10 dB).starts 50-95% by shape; +10% on an FPV channel; +10% if continuous; halved if bursty; halved if on a WiFi channel and not continuous

                        
Wideband digital link (shape)Flat top, 10-40 MHz wide (crest <5 dB, edge drop <8 dB). WiFi looks identical.capped at 60%; +25% only if continuous; x0.8 intermittent; halved if bursty or on a WiFi channel

                        
Calibrated signaturePeak on a channel learned from YOUR drone with calibrate_detector.py, shape inside its learned ranges.75-90% (set by how it tested)

                        

                        
Below 50% nothing is reported (it stays visible in the Raw Data log). 50-60% = low, 60-80% = medium, above 80% = high.
                        Every detection has a "Why it says this" list showing exactly which of these applied.


                        
Site baseline and dual-band correlation

                        
Site baseline (optional, recommended): 5-15 minutes recorded with no drones, saved per sensor. Signals on
                        frequencies busy ≥80% of that time are a known fixed emitter (confidence x0.3), 20-80% common here (x0.7), ≤2%
                        new (+5%). It also replaces the startup learning.

                        
Dual-band (Chris's F7/F9): new activity appearing on 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz in the same cycles (≥60/80/95% of
                        them: +5/10/15%) and their levels moving together (correlation >0.4/0.6/0.8: +3/7/10%). If neither band matches a rule
                        on its own but F7 ≥80%, it reports "Dual-band link activity" - at most medium, because a new dual-band WiFi device
                        could do the same.


                        
3. Duty cycle and airtime

                        
Duty cycle: in what % of sweeps over the last 30 s was this frequency busy (measured on the middle half of the signal,
                        so its weak edges don't drag it down). Airtime: within one 6.5 ms capture, what % of the time was it actually
                        transmitting. A video link is on every sweep and transmits ~100% of the time - continuous. WiFi comes and goes and sends
                        packets with gaps - bursty or intermittent. Until 8 sweeps of history exist it shows collecting.


                        
4. Tracks, positions and alerts

                        
The same drone seen in several sweeps, or by several sensors, is one entry: ACTIVE while seen, GONE after ~12 s
                        of silence. With 3+ sensors hearing it, the position is estimated from how loudly each hears it (tens of metres accuracy -
                        see the ± circle; "OUTSIDE, to the SW" when it is beyond the sensor ring). "Signal rising / falling" is relative to the
                        loudest sensor.


                        
5. What it can't do (yet)

                        
  • Name a drone's make/model - that needs decoding Remote ID / DJI DroneID.
    • 
                        
  • Tell two drones on the same band apart - they merge into one entry.
    • 
                        
  • Bands not swept: 433 MHz, 915 MHz, 1.2/1.3 GHz.
